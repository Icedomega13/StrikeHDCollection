# Ultimate edition: Amiga assets

The user wants the best of each version together, with what the community considered better in each kept. Same rule as the ROMs: **no Amiga data ships**. The player points the game at their own Amiga disk images, and the assets are extracted into a pack folder.

## What the Amiga disks hold (extracted 2026-10-23)
Source: 3 ADFs (Desert Strike: Return to the Gulf, 1993). Each disk has a valid but **empty** AmigaDOS volume: the game uses EA's own track loader. Data blocks are found by signature; most are compressed with **Rob Northen ProPack** (RNC methods 1 and 2), and every block is verified by its CRC.

Tools are in `tools/amiga/`:
- `rnc.py`: RNC decompressor, a port of lab313ru/rnc_propack_source (permissive licence).
- `iff.py`: IFF ILBM and ANIM (op 5) decoder, with EHB/HAM support.
- `adf.py`: AmigaDOS reader (not needed for these disks).

| Content | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ProTracker modules (`M.K.`) | 5 | `death`, `winlevel`, `loading`, `end`, `obj`, plus raw `M.K.` blocks still to check (2 on disk 1, 6 on disk 2) |
| Digitized samples (RNC method 2, raw signed 8-bit) | 47 unique | sound effects and speech; the real playback rates are still to be read from the game code |
| IFF ILBM pictures | 5 | briefing room, general, soldier, Pilar Cloud news report, loading screen |
| IFF ANIM cutscenes | 2 | dungeon execution scene (56 frames, 352x290), one of 16 frames |
| Hunk-format blocks | 25 | game code and per-level data (redrawn graphics), not yet examined |

Two RNC headers with plausible sizes still fail to unpack (disk 1 `$6D7A8`, disk 2 `$6CF94`); many other "failures" are false matches inside other data.

## What the community preferred (1993 reviews and later retrospectives)
**Amiga** (reviewed at 85-93%, and called better than the original by most magazines):
- far better sound: digitized, directional effects in place of the Mega Drive's "lacklustre" sound (Amiga Computing, CU Amiga, Amiga Power);
- digitized, "meaty" explosions; shadows and smoke from the 64-colour Extra Half-Brite mode;
- graphics touched up or redrawn, with extra animation frames for the helicopter (CU Amiga);
- an onboard computer voice warning of low supplies, plus some speech;
- a cinematic, animated intro and presentation; better title and interlude music; the intro's James Brown samples and the level-complete tune were praised.

**Mega Drive:**
- controls: the 3-button pad handles the actions best; the Amiga's mouse control was "virtually unplayable" and joystick-plus-keyboard awkward;
- speed: the Amiga occasionally slows down, and some sounds glitch or cut out;
- the original content: the Amiga version's content is toned down (Amiga Joker);
- no disk swapping; the Amiga needed 3 disks and saved by passwords.

## Plan: Genesis engine, Amiga presentation
The game stays the recompiled Mega Drive version: its controls, speed and content, plus our saves and widescreen. Mixed in from the Amiga, each with a setting:
1. **Sound effects and speech:** watch the 68000's sound requests to the Z80 driver, play the matching Amiga sample, and silence that one Genesis effect. Add the computer voice warnings (low fuel/armour) from the Amiga samples, driven by the HUD values. The mapping is made with the user, with a side-by-side test screen.
2. **Music:** intro/title, level complete, death, ending and loading, played from the MODs by a built-in ProTracker player (to write; small). Gameplay has no music on either version.
3. **Intro and cutscenes:** Amiga pictures and animations in place of, or alongside, the Genesis screens, shown 4:3.
   - **User decision (2026-10-23): the game-picker intros stay exactly as they are.** The launcher's live Genesis intro shuffle (and the planned follow-the-selection background) is unchanged. An Amiga intro, if used, plays only when Desert Strike itself starts, after it's picked.
4. **Later:** Amiga explosion and helicopter frames as HD-pack art. Both versions use about 320-pixel-wide low resolution, so the art fits at 1x and can be upscaled.

## Built so far (2026-10-23)
- **Editions** (Settings > Audio > Edition): Genesis / Amiga / Remaster (default) / Custom. A preset sets the per-feature options; changing one of them by hand switches to Custom (`apply_edition` in `src/host/main.c`). Features: Sound Effects, Music and Intro (each Genesis/Amiga). Remaster uses all three from the Amiga: its effects were the clear favourite, and its title/interlude music and cinematic intro were rated above the Genesis's. Explosions will be added to the same presets.
- **Amiga disks:** Settings > Audio > Add Amiga Disks copies the player's `.adf` files into `<settings>/amiga/`. `src/host/amiga.c` finds the RNC blocks, unpacks them (C port of the RNC algorithm, CRC-checked) and keeps the audio blocks: **63 samples** (47 RNC method 2, 16 stored as method 0, `"RNC\0"` + size + raw data), identified by a content hash so any copy of the disks works. One block (disk 2 `$6CF94`, an instrument of the briefing song) fails its CRC on the user's disks but decodes to full length; it is used for the song only, never as an effect.
- **Sound effect swap:** a `hints.json` patch at Desert `$0088FC` calls `g_sfx_hook(id)` before the game's effect call (`jsr $F068`). When it returns 1 the Genesis effect is skipped and `src/host/sfxmix.c` plays the mapped Amiga sample: 8 voices, linear interpolation, and a repeated effect restarts its own voice. With no hook (tests) the patch does nothing; lockstep still matches 5/5 for Desert.
- **Mapping:** `amiga_sfx.ini` in the settings folder, one `<game> <id> <sample> <rate>` per line. It is made with the **Sound Lab** (Settings > Audio): while flying, the last effects are listed. `[` and `]` pick the Amiga sample for the newest one, `-` and `=` change its pitch by a semitone, `0` goes back to Genesis, and `\` replays it. Once the user's mapping is done, it can ship as the default: ids and hashes only, no Amiga data.
- **Desert effect ids** found from play logs (`DS_SFX_LOG` in `ds_test`, which also logs the HUD values):
  - 0 chain gun;
  - 2 Hydra launch, 31 Hydra hit;
  - 14 Hellfire launch, 10 Hellfire hit;
  - 22 out of ammo;
  - 12/13 taking hits;
  - 17/25 destroyed;
  - 18 low armour;
  - 5 take-off, 6 menu.
- The Amiga effect table is built at run time, so the original pitches are set by ear in the Sound Lab instead.

### Music (2026-10-23)
- **Modules rebuilt from the disks** (`rebuild_modules` in `src/host/amiga.c`): ProTracker `M.K.` songs sit inside the method-1 program blocks. loading and obj carry their instruments; the rest (in program blocks, or standalone blocks like death, winlevel and end) have only the header and patterns, and each instrument is the separate audio block of exactly that length (lengths are unique across the disks). All 11 rebuilt in C byte-identical to the Python rebuild: datitle, story1, industry, main, news, briefing, loading, obj, winlevel, death, end.
- **Player:** `src/host/modplay.c`, 4 channels, PAL periods, the usual effects (0-7, 9-F, E1/E2/E6/E9-EE), soft stereo. Mixed in `pump_audio` with the effects mixer.
- **Hook:** the `$00890E` patch now honours a result: 1 skip the Genesis request, 2 replace it with 0 (stop the Genesis music while the module plays). `g_music_ctx` = 1 marks the campaign briefing request (patch at `$005E36`; the song comes from the ROM table `$95C` by campaign: 117, 104, 4, 109), since campaign 1's briefing reuses the title song.
- **Genesis music ids** (from `DS_SFX_DEBUG` runs): 105 EA logo + title (one Z80 song that runs through the title), 117 title (restarted after Start), 9 story "The Middle East", 107 the refinery, 3 the madman's bunker (also the game over, after the life-lost request 7280), 118 campaign complete (scene record `$8C6`), 2 scene `$902` (not identified), 0 / 100 stop.
- **Default mapping** (`music_defaults` in `src/host/main.c`): 105 and 117 datitle; 9 story1; 107 industry; 3 main; game over (key 300003) death; briefings (100000 + id) briefing; 118 winlevel. `amiga_music.ini` overrides it (`<game> <key> <module|genesis>`); the Sound Lab's `,` and `.` step the module for the last song (Genesis first) and show it on the Song line. Unmapped requests stop the module and play the Genesis music.
- Settings > Audio: **Music** (Genesis/Amiga), **Amiga Music Volume**.

### Intro (2026-10-23)
- The program block holding datitle also holds the three title photographs (IFF ILBM, 16/32 colours): the Apache over jungle, the same in sepia, the sunset silhouette. Decoded in C (`collect_pictures`, ByteRun1, EHB), cropped to 352x224 (past that is overscan).
- When Desert Strike starts (never in the game picker, whose intros are unchanged), `amiga_intro()` shows them with fades and a slow push-in, about 22 s, over datitle. Any key, button or click fades out. The Genesis EA logo/title then asks for song 105, which is mapped to datitle, so the song carries on uninterrupted into the Genesis title.
- Settings > Audio: **Intro** (Genesis/Amiga). Tests: `DS_INTRO=1` shows it under `--frames`.
- The Amiga's animated story scenes (ANIM op 5: the dungeon, the briefing room with the general) are decoded by `tools/amiga/iff.py` but not used yet; the Genesis story screens play, with the Amiga story songs.

### The game's own effects, and extras (2026-10-23, second pass)
- **Finding:** the 47 method-2 "samples" were nearly all **song instruments** (each now labelled `song:instrument` in the Sound Lab). The Amiga game's real effects are one bank: disk 2 block `$0C1930`, a one-hunk program file whose data starts with 26 records `{ offset.l, period_lo.w, period_hi.w, length_words.w }`. `collect_effect_bank` loads them first, as **fx 1-26**, with the game's own rate (`2 x 3546895 / (lo + hi)`, capped at 28 kHz; fx 12/13 have periods below the hardware minimum, so their pitch needs an ear). The Sound Lab uses that rate when one is picked. Total now 89 samples. Spectrograms: fx 2 looks like a rotor loop, fx 3/4/16 explosions, **fx 25 and 26 look like speech** (harmonic, gliding, syllables). Audition copies: `Downloads\DesertStrikeAmiga\effects\fxNN.wav` on the user's PC (from their disks; not shipped).
- **More Genesis songs identified** (the scene dispatcher at `$005D62`, d0 = outcome from `$FF10E6`): 2 = mission failed (outcome `$A`, scene `$902`) mapped to death; the ending is scene `$16` (song 105, title) then `$1A` (song 108, then 10 on page 12 of the list at `$2C30`) mapped to end.
- **Music hook fix:** replacing a request now *skips* it (result 1) unless a Genesis song is still on, when it asks for a stop (2). Turning the campaign briefing's request into a stop hung the briefing (the stop path waits for vblanks).
- **Amiga Extras** (Settings > Audio, in the Edition presets): events the Genesis has no sound for, fired by the host watcher: 1001 objective complete (default: the Amiga `obj` jingle at 60 %, with the game's audio ducked to 45 % while it plays), 1002 mission start, 1003 low fuel (crossing 25 %, re-armed by refuelling), 1004 life lost. 12 s cooldown each. Any of them can be given a sample in the Sound Lab (they appear in its list with a "+"), which replaces the default; the voice lines are meant for these. Verified headless: mission start and objective events, the jingle and ducking, the briefing no longer hangs.

### Default effect mapping (2026-10-23, from the Codex audio pass)
The user had Codex (on their PC) label the 26 effects with AudioSet (AST, CLAP), Whisper and wav2vec2; its report is `Downloads\DesertStrikeAmiga\effects\AUDIO_HANDOFF_TO_MAIN_DEV_2026-10-23.md`, with a review player in `audio-results\audio_review.html`. Speech: fx 6 "Over here!", fx 7 "Help!" (rescue calls), fx 25 "Warning", fx 26 "Mission complete"; fx 10/11 cries; fx 5 beep; fx 2 rotor; fx 3/16/19 explosions. All event matches are low confidence (no one listened), so they ship as **defaults the Sound Lab overrides** (`sfx_defaults` in `src/host/main.c`, ids and hashes only): 0 chain gun fx 1, 2/14 launches fx 4, 31 Hydra hit fx 19, 10 Hellfire hit fx 3, 12/13 hits fx 20, 17/25 destroyed fx 16, 6 menu fx 5, 1001 objective fx 26 ("Mission complete": Desert's numbered objectives are its missions) plus the jingle, 1003 low fuel fx 25. Not mapped: 18 low armour (repeats too often for a voice), 22 empty ammo, 1002, 1004. Defaults are not written to `amiga_sfx.ini`; editing one in the Sound Lab makes it the player's own, and `0` now writes an explicit `genesis` line so a default can be turned off. Rescue calls (fx 6/7) need a trigger (an MIA near the helicopter) not yet found.

- **Enemy weapon sounds (2026-10-24 fix):** the enemy weapon table at `$095F86` (30-byte entries; +2 projectile, +$12 fire sound, played by `$00D684`) shows 29 is the enemy guns (entries 14-17 fire the chain gun's bullet), 13 the missile sites/launchers (8-10, 13) and 30 the rocket launchers (11, 12). The earlier defaults had 29 on the missile sound (so every enemy gun sounded like a Hellfire) and 13 on the hit bang. Then 29 = fx 1, 13 and 30 = fx 4 (refined the same day, next point), 21 unmapped (it comes from `$006A0E`/`$009CEA`, not a weapon). `DS_SFX_CALLER=1` logs each request's caller and a0/a4.
- **Weapon fire as the Amiga plays it (2026-10-24):** the Amiga campaign files (disk 3, e.g. `004DBA`) carry the same weapon table, 44-byte entries; the game exe's fire routine (`$00D79E`) plays effect `+$20` with pitch selector `+$14` (0 = the bank entry's first period, 1 = the second, -1 = random between) through the effect player `$0148E8` (8 word arguments: effect, volume, channel mode, ...). So the Hydra and the Hellfire are both fx 4, the Hydra at the second (lower-pitched) period, the Hellfire at the first; missile sites fire fx 4 at the Hydra pitch; the four enemy guns use fx 12, 13, 14 and 19. `play_amiga_weapon` in `main.c` reads the Genesis entry from a4 at the sound request and applies this (`amiga_weapons[]`) while that id's mapping is the default; `amiga_sample.per_lo/per_hi` hold the bank's periods.
- **Sound Labeler (`tools/amiga/sound_labeler.html`, also `Downloads\DesertStrikeAmiga\effects\Sound Labeler.html` beside the WAVs):** plays fx 1-26, keeps the user's names in the browser, lets them pick an fx (or the Genesis sound) for each game event and saves an `amiga_sfx.ini` for the settings folder.

### Rotor, rescue calls (2026-10-23, tester feedback)
- **Rotor:** Desert's rotor is YM channel 5 (index 4), started by effect 6 when the helicopter appears on the frigate (so 6 is "rotor start", not "menu": its fx 5 default was removed, it had silenced the rotor) and 5 at take-off. Found with a per-channel level meter added to ymfm (`ymfm_chan_level`, and `ymfm_chan_mute` to leave channels out; local patch in `third_party/ymfm/src/ymfm_opn.cpp`, output unchanged when nothing is muted). With Amiga effects on and id 1010 assigned (default fx 2), `rotor_frame` mutes that channel while it sounds and plays the Amiga rotor instead.
  - **The Amiga's rotor routine** (disk 1 program block `$02EF42`, hunk 0 `$0073C4`, run every frame): the rotor sample (effect bank entry 1) loops on Paula channels 0 and 1 (left and right). A shared base period (`$177EA`) eases from `$1E7` = 487 (idle) to `$133` = 307 (flying): -5 a frame spinning up, +1 a frame running down, by helicopter state (`$17F28`). Per channel: period - 1.5 x |speed| (pitch rises with speed), +-2 x turn rate (left and right pull apart in pitch while turning, the shifting doubled sound), +-1/4 of another motion value; volume 23 + speed/8, panned by the helicopter's screen x. Started at `$0074E6`.
  - The host copy (`rotor_frame`): two looping voices, panned left and right (`sfx_loop_pan`), period 487 -> 307 when the helicopter is off the deck (height at +8 of its position record above 12), minus 54 x speed (velocity +$18/+$1C, full speed ~1.37 px/frame), +-14 x turn (+$14 high word / 32); level about the Genesis rotor's. User report that started it: "the sound changes pitch with the speed of the helicopter, it's not just one sound it's multiple".
  - The Amiga rotor stays on over the game's pause screen (the Genesis rotor keeps sounding there; before, the Genesis one came back): its context lasts from the playfield for as long as channel 5 keeps sounding.
- **Enemy fire:** effects 21 and 29 play when no player ammo is used (likely the AA missile sites; 14 is the player's Hellfire, confirmed by the Hellfire count). The user's comparison with Amiga gameplay video says the Amiga enemy missile launch is the fx 4 sound, so 21 and 29 default to fx 4 (Sound Lab to correct).
- **Rescue calls:** `watch_rescue` finds the pause map's rescue entries ("MIA's" etc.) and checks every unit's distance to the helicopter: within 220 px "Help!" (1005, fx 7), within 90 px "Over here!" (1006, fx 6), one call per 7 s. Part of Amiga Extras.

### Cutscenes (2026-10-23)
- **Amiga side** (`collect_anims` in `src/host/amiga.c`): every screen-sized IFF ILBM and ANIM (op 5, double-buffered byte vertical delta) in the program blocks, plus the wide scrolling strips, decoded to palette-indexed frames: 27 on the user's disks (story scenes, briefing room, deck, base at sunset with the building blown up (38 frames), dungeon (56), general (16), embassy, cockpit, ceremony, crash, loading ...). Ids are content hashes ("c" + FNV-64). ANIM reltime is 1 jiffy everywhere (the cutscene programs pace them), so frames step at about 12 a second.
- **Genesis side:** every cutscene and briefing screen goes through the scene player `$005994` with a1 = a scene record (+$A song, +$12 picture, +$16 second picture, +$1A script). Two `hints.json` patches set `g_scene_rec` at `$0059C8` and clear it at `$005D0C`. The story is the record list at `$25E6` played by `$0FFA8E`; the outcome screens are picked by `$005D62` (d0 from `$FF10E6`: 2 campaign won `$8C6`, 6 game over `$8E4`, `$A` mission failed `$902`, `$E` briefing `$920`/`$93E`, `$12` per-mission `$FF10F0`, `$16`/`$1A` the ending `$2C5E`/`$2BE4`).
- **The swap** (`scene_swaps` in `src/host/main.c`, Settings > Audio > Cutscenes, in the Edition presets): the Genesis picture's rectangle is covered by the matching Amiga picture, animated where the Amiga animates it; the Genesis text, timing and music handling stay. Story pictures sit at y 48-160 on both machines; the Amiga's are 352 wide, so they spill 16 px into the side bars. Done, which covers every picture the Genesis cutscenes use (a scan of the ROM for scene records finds 11): dungeon (anim), bunker, Kilbaba, general (anim, looping), news report, Apache on the frigate, refinery (sky and ground strips scrolling at two speeds, as on the Amiga), the briefing room (board and officer, in the Genesis's portrait panel; also the mission-failed and "Congratulations" screens, which reuse that picture), campaign won (the Apache on the deck), game over (the wreck in the desert) and the ending ceremony. The between-campaign dialogues (records near `$0FFB02`) reuse the story pictures. Not used yet (no Genesis scene to put them in): the base at sunset (the building blown up), the embassy, the bunker entrance, the cockpit, the loading screen.
- **Briefing room overlays (bobs):** the briefing program (disk 2 `$000800`) blits 13 masked bobs over the room: cigarette smoke (bobs 0-2, every 6 frames, at 143,103), two seated pilots (3-5 at 124,90 and 6-8 at 171,90) and the officer (9-12 at 148,38), each pilot/officer on a random 10-29 frame timer, PAL 50 Hz. `collect_bobs` finds the table through the draw routine (`41F9 <abs> D1FB 30xx`); `scene_actors` in `main.c` replays the timing. The crop was widened so the officer's arm is whole.
- **Amiga VM (`src/host/amiga_vm.c`, 2026-10-23 night):** the other animated scenes are small programs with their own engines (copper lists, double-buffered blits, object scripts), so they run as-is in a minimal Amiga: Musashi 68000 (`harness/musashi`, built into the host with the instruction hook and PMMU off), 2 MB chip RAM, the hunk file loaded at `$10000` with relocations, a stack returning to a `STOP` sentinel, the full area blitter (shifts, masks, modulos, minterms, descending, fill; no line mode), VERTB each frame and copper interrupts at the copper list's line, DMACONR (blitter never busy), VPOSR/VHPOSR from the cycle count (141875 cycles, 313 lines a frame), CIA `$BFE001` and POTGOR as buttons up. Each frame the copper list is walked for bitplane pointers, BPLCON0, modulos, DDF/DIW and colour moves per line (EHB included) to read the screen back (up to 384x290). Address 0 must read 0: the crash program uses `move.w $0.l,$64(a6)` as a blit modulo. `amiga.c` keeps every hunk program that holds an ILBM (`amiga_program_find`, keyed like the bobs by its first picture's hash; 14 on the user's disks). Scenes with `vm` set in `scene_swaps` run their program from the scene's start at 50 frames a second: the frigate (crew at work, program owner `c31C4574AE4C38B0A`), the refinery (flares, `cB757CAFFE3F99720`), game over (burning wreck, a man climbing the hill, `c091FDEF2CF1CF57E`) and the ending ceremony (`c3522000D86C85073`). The briefing program stays black in the VM (it waits on something from the game), so the briefing keeps the bobs. Programs that run fine but have no Genesis scene: the base at sunset with the Apache, the embassy and the bus (two), the cockpit with the fire, the commander's message screen, the options menu, the base fade. `DS_AVM_TEST=<index or owner>:<frames>:<png prefix>` runs one program headless and saves every 25th frame.
- **Tests:** `DS_ROM_POKE=<addr>:<long>` patches ROM data at frame 3000 (after the game's ROM checksum), e.g. `25EE:8E4` puts the game-over scene into the attract story.
- **Tests:** `DS_SCENE_LOG=1` logs each scene's record, pictures and song; `DS_SCENE_SHOTS=<dir>` saves a screenshot 100 frames into each.

- **Still to do:** Z80 digital samples (the Genesis has no speech to replace; the Amiga radio chatter samples could be added as extras), the Amiga-only scenes (run in the VM, need a place in the Genesis flow), the computer voice warnings, and Jungle/Urban hook points.
